# Comparative craft sources

This is a small checked starting set, not a claim to an exhaustive film-history education. Read the relevant material and the actual reviewed work. Add source-pinned research when the cultural, literary or aesthetic question needs more. A famous name or theory is never evidence of a film's success. Do not treat extracts, stills or the following essays as a viewing of those films.

## Relational dramatic craft without one acting doctrine

[Satyajit Ray: a moral attitude](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/interviews/satyajit-ray-moral-attitude), Andrew Robinson, Sight and Sound, 3 September 2013: a compiled primary interview. Relevant passages discuss critical independence (moral-attitude question), social surroundings beyond plot (Company Limited), establishing relationships through observation/calculation, and Ray's distinction between a humanist label and form, rhythm, composition and light. Use this to ask how the world and relations act on a scene. Ray's own preference is one artistic position; do not make his staging advice universal or adopt the interview's gender generalizations.

## Character force through material action and offscreen relations

[A Man Escaped: Quintessential Bresson](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2628-a-man-escaped-quintessential-bresson), Tony Pipolo, 25 March 2013: a secondary critical essay. Its analysis connects restrained performance with concrete tasks, objects, framing, sound and offscreen space. This supplies a counterexample to requiring demonstrative emotion or biographical exposition. It does not prove that any neutral face is expressive or license prescribing Bresson's method for every work. Claims about the film here are attributed to Pipolo, not independently observed by this review team.

[Bordwell and Thompson Listen to A Man Escaped](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2705-david-bordwell-and-kristin-thompson-listen-to-a-man-escaped), Criterion, 27 March 2013: introduction to a Film Art sound essay, identifying nonsimultaneous commentary as an analytical topic. The page supports investigating image/sound temporal relations, not detailed claims about an unplayed video excerpt or its mix.

## Quiet world, absence and retrospective relation

[The enigmatic pillow shots of Yasujiro Ozu](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/enigmatic-pillow-shots-yasujiro-ozu), Leigh Singer, originally 12 December 2016, updated 8 September 2023: secondary essay with identified film examples. It discusses transitional everyday spaces, family memory and life continuing after loss. Use as a counterexample to treating every still or empty interval as disposable. A vase's fixed symbolic meaning, lens norm or universal Japanese aesthetic does not follow; interpretations of absence remain argued.

## Work-specific literature, contrasting values

Read the designated source in its pinned edition. A character's philosophical speech need not be the author's final thesis. Source narrative form, irony and uncertainty deserve attention alongside plot and ethics. The relevant existing literary-source-analysis and philosophical-core Skills preserve fact/interpretation separation.

[The Gift of the Magi](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/7256), O. Henry, Project Gutenberg edition 7256; [plain text](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7256/pg7256.txt): primary literary source. Its limited money, objects, reciprocal sacrifice, misread entrance and comic reversal permit a different model of emotional depth from a nihilistic confession. Study that difference from the text rather than imposing philosophical monologues or a solemn palette.
