# Cinematography and lighting before shot execution

Consume readiness, Director intent, route, film color script, scene design and
costume states before finalizing camera/light. Establish intent → character/DPD →
performance → blocking → camera. [Preproduction](../../../docs/cinematic-preproduction.md)
uses LightingScript owned here, not a new lighting skill.

Per scene: dramatic light intent; motivated source IDs already in the scene
packet; key direction, fill, contrast, value hierarchy, character readability,
background separation, practicals, atmosphere, daytime logic, relationship to
color key, continuity in/out and route-specific risks. At night, use plausible
sources and designed bounce/separation to let a decision be seen. Physical
plausibility does not demand unreadable blackness or flat incidental light.
Avoid unexplained sources and automatic glamour treatment; practical neon or gold
light is eligible only when supported by this period, location and dramatic intent.
Compare costume value and material response against each dramatic light state.

Trace only the time and location transitions actually present in the screenplay.
For example, if a scene moves from a night interior through outdoors to dawn,
account for doorway spill, sky fill and changed direction. This is a
NON_NORMATIVE_EXAMPLE, not a required sequence or blue palette.
Camera plan: philosophy, dominant scale, height logic, lens family, motivated
movement, depth, axis, restraint on heroic privilege and environment framing.
CG virtual freedom does not permit unmotivated orbit, equipment display or a
camera that destroys geography. No exact light positions, renderer settings,
provider parameters or production freeze here.

Project the scene plan into existing CinematicShotSpec only after performance and
blocking. Preserve SourceSoundIntent/native priority, DPD authority and original
source dialogue. Unknown real visual quality stays UNKNOWN. LightingScript and
text design reviews do not constitute CG Lookdev or artistic approval.
