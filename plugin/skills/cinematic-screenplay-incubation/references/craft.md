# Twenty creative controls within six modules

Use the questions to make creative choices. Evidence lives in the Bible or review; the screenplay body contains action and dialogue. A PASS needs an actual scene/line/state example. N/A needs a scope reason. Historical credibility is evidence-based; private behavior remains an interpretation.

## Historical & Logic

**01 Historical Truth Graph.** For consequential events record actor, time, location, cause, motivation, knownInformation, constraint, decision, consequence, evidence and certainty. Unknown motivation stays unknown. Link causes by event ID; chronology alone is not causation. Preserve collective/office attribution when no named actor is evidenced.

**02 Timeline & Geography.** Track characters, units, city control, routes and the interval in which travel/messages could occur. Distinguish destination from arrival and dispatch from receipt. Use order/elapsed intervals where precision is unavailable; never let simultaneous scenes put one person in two places. Geographic direction must constrain decisions.

**03 Certainty & Adaptation Position.** Use Confirmed, Probable, Disputed, Later Tradition, Dramatic Reconstruction. Confirmed means sufficiently corroborated for this bounded adaptation, not infallible chronicles. For conflicts retain alternatives, selected reading, source basis and what remains uncertain. A translated/paraphrased line is not a direct historical quotation.

**04 Historical → Dramatic Causality.** Show information acquired → judgment → resistance → choice → cost → consequence. A historical event may inform several scenes; several events may form one necessary scene. A fictional clerk/object can embody a process, but cannot become the unsupported cause of a historical decision. Preserve both scene necessity and the historical dramatization-deletion test; they test different levels.

## Character & Relationship

**05 Behavioral Model.** Specify how pressure, disagreement, humiliation, uncertainty and rank change tactics. “Steady” may mean asking for missing facts before speaking; it does not mean identical calm speeches. Rigid judgment may reinterpret counterevidence instead of shouting. Use behavior appropriate to this person and available sources, not these examples as fixed presets.

**06 Invariants.** Give each principal a few stable rules with permitted exceptions and costs. Pair normal behavior with pressure, possible deviation, break condition and recovery when relevant. At each scene ask whether this action violates a rule; a justified breaking point needs accumulated pressure and later consequences. Do not explain a drift by appending a new trait after the fact.

**07 Capability Boundary.** Qualitatively distinguish military, strategic, tactical, political, administrative, social, emotional-control, risk, command-presence and physical-combat ability. Unknown remains unknown; do not invent numeric rankings. State how one strength becomes costly under changed information or institutions. These military/political axes are available dimensions, not compulsory roles; mark irrelevant axes N/A and use the capabilities actually at stake in this subject.

**08 Relationship & Power.** Separate formal, actual, military, political, information and imperial-access power; track trust, fear, resentment, dependency and debt only where relevant. An old favor does not guarantee current obedience. Authority should alter access, timing, silence, requests and who may refuse whom.

**09 Knowledge / Information Gap.** Keep world truth, audience disclosure, a person's information and a person's belief distinct. Record source/channel and receipt moment. A false belief can motivate action only with a plausible cause. The author knowing an ending never lets a character predict it as fact. Do not hide normally shared information merely to create a twist.

## Story & Rhythm

**10 Causal Story Chain.** Connect scenes through therefore/but rather than calendar succession. Perform the delete-scene test: name what later action becomes unmotivated or illegible. Atmosphere can be useful, but redundant atmosphere is not causal necessity.

**11 Continuity Ledger.** Update only changed facts after each scene: location, physical state, office, life/death, army/city, relationship/power, learned information, false beliefs, promises, orders, key objects and unresolved setups. The next input inherits the prior output. Offscreen travel or dispatch still needs a transition record.

**12 Foreshadow / Payoff.** Keep setup, status, expectedPayoff, actualPayoff and expiry. Close, deliberately leave open or remove a setup. A payoff must use something available before it solves a problem; no late information miracle. An open dramatic question is not necessarily a promised plot device.

**13 Dramatic Delta / Pacing.** For each scene identify information, relationship, power, goal, risk, emotional or physical change. Compare episode tension, information release, escalation, breathing, climax and aftermath. Flag consecutive unchanged scenes, lectures, meetings or battles; fixed words, shots or seconds cannot make rhythm pass.

**14 Emotional Curve / Budget.** Reserve peaks by contrast. Establish pressure, temporary relief or hope, loss of options, choice and aftermath. Choose expression from the material: exuberance, confrontation, humor, bodily stillness or an outcry may each be earned. Quiet expression has no automatic artistic priority. Record what the audience has had time to absorb; never require a prescribed emotion per scene.

**15 Historically Bounded Arc.** Change knowledge, belief, relationship or understanding of position without changing documented outcomes. A capable person may learn too late, remain mistaken or preserve a value at a cost. No compulsory redemption, psychological growth or invented private cause of history.

## War & Cinematic Expression

**16 War Dramaturgy.** Macro: terrain, deployment, objective, command chain, reserve, flank, retreat and supply. Meso: unit, formation, advance, morale, casualty, cavalry pressure and infantry response. Micro: breath, feet, weapon weight, horse reaction, visibility, sound, fear and local command. Return from bodily experience to unit consequence and strategic change. If not depicting a battle, explicitly limit what can be judged; do not add spectacle for coverage.

**17 Army Personality.** Training, discipline, experience, morale, commander trust, formation habits, risk, fatigue, supply and cohesion change what soldiers actually do. Different bodies of troops do not become distinct merely through uniform labels. Do not generalize ethnicity into inherent courage or competence.

**18 Show, Don't Tell.** For each explanatory emotional line, compare action, speech, object, environment and silence; select what serves this moment, not a fixed preference for silence. Retain speech when it performs a necessary interpersonal action. A visual gesture must be observable and physically possible. Avoid filling every moment with symbolic props.

**19 Texture / Motif.** Choose a narrative texture suited to the line of action and only a few recurring objects/sensory patterns. Each recurrence should change use or meaning, not merely decorate. Vary spaces and actions because pressure demands it, not to satisfy a visual-location quota.

## Scene & Dialogue / downstream input

**20 Voice / Subtext / Performance Intent.** Differentiate sentence length, directness, formality, metaphor, military/political vocabulary, emotional restraint, silence, question and threat style. For material lines separate surfaceMeaning, trueIntent, subtext, powerPosition, speechStrategy and emotionalLeak. Supply sparse surfaceEmotion/hiddenEmotion, target, intent, tempo, pause, force, breathing and restraint as provider-neutral creative advice; later direction/projection owns its concrete contract. No control metadata in audience dialogue. Read a scene with names removed: if voices exchange without a change in tactics, revise the affected lines.
