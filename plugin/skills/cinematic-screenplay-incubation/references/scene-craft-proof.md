# Whole-scene craft proof

Read when repairing a complete dramatic passage or calibrating writing/directing quality. The deliverable is readable action and dialogue; use only the decisions that change it. This is a method, not a required tone, scene template or additional schema.

## Preserve history; reopen authored expression

Separate evidenced events, outcomes, actor attribution and disputes from the adopted screenplay and from earlier AI wording. Keep the former constrained and retain adopted versions. Within authorized candidate revision, reconsider language, scene organization, information order and character interpretation. A stored `mustKeep` is binding for production from that source; it is not by itself evidence that the user forbade an alternative draft. Honor an explicit user lock; retain the source and label an alternative where appropriate. Private psychology and small invented actions remain adaptation choices, never recovered facts. Put mappings in an appendix, not every line of the screenplay.

## Write the contest before explaining it

Let each participant try to obtain something from a specific listener. Give resistance a practical reason and enough force to change the other person's tactic. A subordinate can ask a consequential operational question without becoming a modern moral commentator. A persuader who offers a workable alternative should respond to the actual refusal, not repeat the initial offer in new words. Do not require all opponents to be equally right.

Read the complete scene without names: distinguish attention, vocabulary, sentence shape and tactic, not merely ornate versus plain. Keep extended speech when its accumulation acts on these listeners; preserve orders, brief answers or silence when they accomplish something else. Restraint is not compulsory brevity. Literary language does not require everyone to discuss fate or the state. Cut a beautiful line if removing it changes neither the listener's options nor the speaker's exposure.

## Make the claim meet an action

When speech promises an observable result, show its fulfillment, deliberate limitation or refusal. Do not let a response certify something the scene never demonstrates. When a gesture carries meaning, establish its practical use first: what is being done, by whom, and why the other party accepts or resists it. A later line may change the meaning of an already real action. Repetition is useful only if it changes that relation.

For action passages, make the route, obstacle and opportunity recognizable before using them. Follow selected decisive actions through approach, contact, result and what another person can now do. Allow an effective opponent to change the next problem. Consecutive fights need different viewing tasks; preserve actual local achievement even when the wider problem remains. These questions also apply to nonviolent action, without forcing conflict or spectacle into every scene.

## Read, criticize, revise, hand off

Read all relevant original Scene and Shot expression before comparing; an old camera/action note may already contain the supposed innovation. Retain full source scope and hashes. Do not manufacture a weak old draft or count added length as improvement. A finding names a sentence/action, its reader-facing consequence and the smallest useful change. Preserve a substantive before/after; stop iterating when the next pass has no useful new intervention. Author judgment, independent textual critique, user acceptance and observed AV quality are different evidence.

Select a few decisions for the existing Cinematic Intent sidecar: intent → source passage → performance/space realization → coverage/edit obligation → future observable result. Use existing priorities and source fingerprints. Candidate text owns candidate wording; DPD extracts objective, target and tactic; direction realizes behavior and camera; design supplies appearance and usable space; finishing consumes reviewed meaning and actual material. They cannot silently write rival dialogue or psychology. A text-only candidate stays outside formal Scene/Shot writes and paid production. No new DirectorBook entity is necessary.
