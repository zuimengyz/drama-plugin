# Direct the encounter before its coverage

Start with the selected complete scene: whose situation the audience enters, what
the people are trying to accomplish, what changes between them, and what the
audience discovers or continues waiting for. Develop a whole-scene proposition
before choosing individual views. A proposition can sustain a ritual or unresolved
relationship; it need not manufacture a turn in every shot.

Host selects the proposition and its meaningful alternatives. Blocking explores
the encounter in one shared space; Camera explores how views disclose or withhold
it; Editorial participates now, proposing where changing view changes experience.
Provisional coverage is enough for this exchange. Do not wait for frozen Shots
before asking Editorial, or concatenate independent department answers.

Use the same selected scene and coverage revision for all contributions. Follow
the action through the space before following it through the cameras. At useful
boundaries ask what remains in progress, what has ended, what is deliberately
omitted, and what draws attention into the next view. Compare incoming and outgoing
images together: world position is different from screen position; a gaze has a
target; a prop has a holder/support and a stage of use. Specify only relations
that matter. A cut can deliberately break time, direction or expectation.

Host resolves contradictory proposals by their audience effect, preserving each
contribution and recording the selected gain and cost in the existing decision
record. A useful contradiction may remain as counterpoint; incompatible execution
states cannot silently coexist. Return a changed dramatic event to screenplay
selection. Return a changed cut, camera or path to its existing owner, then review
the affected neighbors and any later use of the changed state. Reuse the existing
support/decision and SequencePackage/EditorialRhythmPlan mechanisms; no new
department, score, universal Gate or mandatory model call is required.

## Real author and execution handoff

For formal native film children, `hostScreenplay.sceneDirection` supplies the
pinned ordered coverage of the same scene and the current selection. It is shared
creative context, not additional action to perform in this shot. The local packet
retains the exact direction/Canon references; the semantic author view omits them.
Do not silently repair an incompatible neighbor inside a single-shot design.
Host must reconcile the relevant originals and prepare a new version. Existing
children retain their old pins for reproducible recovery.

Project the selected meaning into existing owners: Shot entry/exit and editing
relation; Action phases and physical states; Camera position, height, movement
and end view; Editorial protected events and spatial orientation. Reference duties
say what a prior image can actually establish, especially when unseen geometry
remains unknown. Keep theory, other shots' actions and adoption history outside
the Provider prompt. A concise prompt cannot recover a decision never made.

An authored `FilmShot.transition` describes the relation between shots. Action
phase `START` describes the first unit of an individual shot; it does not by itself
declare a film opening. The native film media planner uses the ordered film
transition for cross-shot CUT/MATCH/CONTINUE. A local task must not infer a film
cut from its file number or equate a reference image with first-frame continuation.
Choose the actual route after the artistic relation is selected.

Before spending, inspect the pairs that contain meaningful uncertainty through
boards, a spatial sketch, rehearsal or scoped external previs. Do not require
Blender for every cut. After generation, compare observed source frames/actions
and usable handles against the intended relation before preparing the receiver.
An official last frame can fall after the useful editorial cut. Use the actual
selected frame/range and its provenance; tail binding alone proves no edit.
Offline design and compiler tests establish consumption, not acting quality,
normal-speed audiovisual continuity or successful media production.
