# Work aesthetic design

Begin with the selected screenplay, literary form and Director intent. Identify
what the spectator should notice, know, misread or continue contemplating: for
example a person's relation to a world that exceeds their immediate purpose.
Compare plausible visual realizations by what each reveals and sacrifices. A
distant person within a legible place can preserve social or spatial relations but
lose a small response; a close view can disclose that response but withhold its
surroundings. Neither distance nor empty space proves loneliness. A commonplace
object acquires dramatic weight through established use, expectation and change,
not by adding a symbolic label or an unexplained insert.

Derive a small coherent set of relations across space, attention, material, light,
color and duration. Surface wear can express particular use and history; pristine
surfaces can equally serve a film. A visible or implied source can unite light
with the lived environment, but naturalism is a choice, not a universal virtue.
Restrained palettes can expose small differences and also suppress necessary
distinctions; saturated color or formal symmetry may better serve another source.
Test the chosen principle against a scene where it would fail. Do not inherit a
named director, palette, focal length, grain or fixed exposure as an aesthetic.
Still examples establish visible relations only, not a character's motive,
offscreen history, motion, camera settings or verified film provenance.

Use the existing WorkDirectingAuthority: grammarRules expresses domain, narrative
reason, appliesWhen, execution, exception and exitCondition; evolution binds
perceptual ownership and change to the relevant scope; aestheticRules states
preferences or exclusions with succeedsWhen/failsWhen. Author only applicable
decisions, not a checklist of mandatory rules. Keep alternative interpretations
in creative deliberation; the selected work original is the shared basis.

Separate durable design from its presentation. Specialized Asset and Prop owners
choose identifiable forms, material/color relationships and justified use history.
Location topology and object construction remain stable across camera views.
Camera, Lighting and Color interpret those originals for each scene's attention,
time and perceptual owner. Preserve continuity of relationships without forcing
every shot into the same exposure or palette. A motivated change can reveal a
previously obscured color without repainting the asset. Asset reference proofs
must retain readable identity, geometry and material; dramatic obscurity belongs
in a shot when its narrative purpose justifies the loss of information.

Direction expresses the selected viewing relationship and its changes in existing
cameraIntent, coverage, entryState/exitState and editingRelation, requesting the
needed professional domains. It does not invent detailed light, color or asset
facts. Department authors resolve those decisions in their existing execution
fields. The final image/video request needs only the current observable result,
not the whole constitution, literary explanation or artist's name. Grain, halation
and final tonal balancing belong to Finishing unless an approved production choice
requires them in the generated image; they cannot repair missing composition.
Learning images remain knowledge evidence unless explicitly selected for a
production reference duty through the existing reference mechanism.
