# Episode Review and Revision

Apply this rubric to the complete Episode draft before persistence. Keep evaluation binary and simple: every listed row is critical because each is required for a formal dramatic unit; one unresolved failure means Review FAIL.

## Domain rubric

| Check | PASS evidence | FAIL signal |
|---|---|---|
| Dramatic job | One pressure-driven objective and change explain why the Episode exists. | The job is a topic, recap, or several unrelated tasks. |
| Script fidelity | Main line, arcs, historical boundary, and structural destination are inherited. | The Episode redesigns the series or drifts from Script. |
| Entry state | Relevant goal, knowledge, relationship, danger, power, and choices are explicit. | The unit starts without a usable dramatic state. |
| Character objective | A central character actively pursues a concrete Episode result. | Characters only observe, explain, or wait. |
| Central conflict | A capable obstacle acts against the objective with real stakes. | Conflict is only discussed or labeled. |
| Progression and escalation | Tactic/counteraction raises cost or narrows options. | The same disagreement repeats without change. |
| Turn | A meaningful event redefines available action. | Nothing changes direction or possibility. |
| Information/character/relationship change | Relevant changes result from action and consequence. | Claimed change is absent or purely descriptive. |
| Exit state | At least one material state differs causally and continuously. | Entry and exit are effectively the same. |
| Ending logic | Hook/resolution is earned by the Episode progression. | A fake cliffhanger is appended to static material. |
| Neighbor continuity | Entry follows prior state and exit supports the next destination. | State, knowledge, motivation, or history jumps without cause. |
| Episode necessity | Deletion would materially damage line, arc, causality, information, or escalation. | The whole series works almost unchanged without it. |
| Short-form rhythm | Pressure begins early and every major beat serves the job. | Recap, exposition, repetition, or overload dominates. |
| Downstream readiness | Scene development can realize the unit without inventing its job or state change. | Only a synopsis exists or detailed Scenes prematurely replace Episode design. |

## Reject common anti-patterns

- **Mechanical Split:** divide Script by length, page count, or chronology.
- **Recap Episode / Exposition Episode:** repeat prior events or teach background instead of changing state.
- **No State Change / Repeated Conflict:** finish where the Episode began after repeating one dispute.
- **Fake Hook:** append suspense unrelated to the Episode's causal progression.
- **Overloaded Episode:** combine several independent dramatic jobs without a coherent center.
- **Detached Episode:** preserve a subplot that does not affect line, arc, information, or escalation.
- **Premature Scene Writing:** substitute detailed Scene dialogue/action for Episode structure.

## Revision and persist gate

Use local revision for a hook, isolated beat, limited pacing, wording, or minor neighbor continuity problem. Re-plan when dramatic job, Script fidelity, objective/conflict, progression, turn, state change, ending logic, necessity, or downstream readiness fails. Label a root series-architecture defect as an upstream Script issue.

After every revision or re-plan, apply the full rubric again. Allow `episode.create_episode` or `episode.save_episode` only after every row passes. Keep alternatives, reasoning, findings, and revision notes temporary; persist only the approved formal dramatic Episode state.
