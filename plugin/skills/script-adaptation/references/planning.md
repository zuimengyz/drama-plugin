# Professional Script Planning

Use this method during the Script `Plan` stage. Plan screen adaptation and whole-series architecture; leave formal Episode entities, detailed Scene writing, and Shot/camera design to their downstream responsibilities.

## 1. Establish the adaptation contract

Record the approved Work foundation that the Script must inherit:

- Historical Scope, ordered Historical Spine, actor hierarchy, Narrative Authority, protagonist alignment, and architecture mappings;
- premise, supported conflict/stakes, thematic question, and ending;
- any optional non-causal character or relationship interpretation;
- supported facts, disputed areas, invention boundary, and forbidden historical changes;
- major story turns, format, audience, tone, scale, and short-form intent.

The Script may redesign screen structure, episode allocation, information reveal, visual storytelling, dialogue strategy, and pacing. It may not silently replace the protagonist, premise, theme, historical boundary, major ending, or other approved Work foundation. Mark such a need as an upstream Work issue.

## 2. Build the main dramatic line

Express the whole-series drive as a causal progression. For each major structural segment, specify its required `spineBeatIds` and:

```text
current historical/story state
→ supported actor action or pressure
→ consequence
→ changed story state
→ next required spine beat
```

Ensure the line is playable without reassigning causal acts. A supported chronology may supply the causal backbone; adaptation adds presentation, pressure, and state change without inventing why the historical outcome occurred.

## 3. Control secondary lines

Use only secondary lines that advance the main line, character arc, theme, climax, or an essential relationship. Define where each line intersects and changes the main progression. Delete, merge, or reduce a line that remains detached. Keep the number appropriate for short-form drama.

## 4. Translate optional interpretation to the screen

Map each approved non-causal Work interpretation, when present, to observable progression:

- action taken under pressure;
- decision and sacrifice;
- failure or consequence;
- changed behavior or relationship;
- final action that demonstrates the arc.

Do not substitute prose claims such as “becomes mature” or “understands responsibility” for visible evidence. Preserve motivation continuity across structural segments.

## 5. Plan information reveal

Track what the audience and major characters know at each important turn. For every consequential reveal, decide who discovers it, how it is discovered, and which decision or relationship it changes.

Prefer action, conflict, discovery, behavior, environment, reaction, and consequence over characters explaining history they both know. Delay or advance information to create meaningful dramatic pressure, not arbitrary confusion.

## 6. Escalate conflict

Progress from an initial problem through complication, higher cost, narrowing options, irreversible decision, crisis, and climax. Make later obstacles harder, costlier, or less reversible rather than repeating the same council debate, accusation, pursuit, or argument.

Check that every escalation changes at least one of goal, danger, knowledge, loyalty, relationship, or available choice.

## 7. Plan episode architecture from required coverage

Script may plan the series architecture without creating Episode entities. For each proposed unit define:

- sequence number and dramatic job;
- entry story/character state;
- central conflict, major turn, or discovery;
- exit state and its causal link to the next unit;
- hook, reversal, decision, crisis, or earned partial resolution.

Allocate by required beat coverage, transition, dramatic task, and state change, not by equal page count, a preset quota, or arbitrary chronology slices. Record `requiredSpineBeatIds` for each proposed unit. Split, merge, or adjust the estimate when a unit is overloaded or a required transition is missing. Detailed single-episode design belongs downstream.

## 8. Shape short-form pacing

Enter pressure early, give each unit material progress, remove repeated exposition and redundant beats, maintain high information and conflict density, and make hooks arise from real change. Do not force platform folklore such as a reversal every thirty seconds or a fixed number of humiliations.

## 9. Make the Script screenable

Express important information through action, behavior, dialogue, visual detail, environment, reaction, and choice. Convert interior prose into something performers and viewers can observe or hear.

Give dialogue a dramatic purpose. Avoid facts both speakers already know, history-textbook lectures, identical voices, and dialogue detached from action. Allow subtext and competing intent; do not force every line into a slogan.

At Script level, define playable progression and representative screen behavior without writing every Scene or specifying framing, lenses, camera movement, or coverage.

## 10. Complete the Script draft contract

The selected plan must support a draft that independently communicates:

- adaptation contract and fidelity constraints;
- whole-series main line and necessary secondary lines;
- visible character/relationship arc progression;
- conflict escalation and information reveal;
- episode architecture with distinct dramatic jobs and state changes;
- pacing, major turns, crisis, climax/payoff, ending, and historical boundary;
- screenability and dialogue strategy.

If Episode development must reinvent why units exist, where the protagonist is heading, or how the whole-series progression works, the Script plan or draft is incomplete.
