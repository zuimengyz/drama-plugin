# Script Review and Revision

Apply this rubric to the complete Script draft before persistence. Treat every row as critical: one unresolved failure means Review FAIL.

## Domain rubric

| Check | PASS evidence | FAIL signal |
|---|---|---|
| Work fidelity | Scope, spine, authority, protagonist alignment, theme, boundary, major turns, and ending inherit the approved Work. | Script silently redesigns the Work or masks an upstream defect. |
| Historical beat coverage | Every indispensable spine beat maps to a Script segment and proposed Episode unit. | A required beat disappears during adaptation. |
| Fact attribution | Actor granularity and primary causal roles remain unchanged. | A collective or higher-authority act is reassigned to a named protagonist. |
| Dramatization non-causality | Compatible invention can be deleted without breaking the historical chain. | Fiction explains an actual historical result. |
| Main line | A clear whole-series pursuit and dramatic question govern all major segments. | The Script is an event list or unrelated collection of episodes. |
| Character motivation | Invented actions have bounded motivation and supported historical actions retain their actual cause. | Motivation fabricates or replaces historical causality. |
| Interpretive progression | Optional Work interpretation becomes observable without carrying historical causality. | Invented growth or relationship change explains an outcome. |
| Secondary lines | Each secondary line serves the main line, arc, theme, or climax. | A subplot is detached, repetitive, or oversized for short form. |
| Conflict escalation | Costs, constraints, and irreversibility increase toward crisis and climax. | Later units repeat the same conflict at the same intensity. |
| Causality | Decisions produce consequences and changed story states. | Chronology rather than choice connects the dramatic units. |
| Information reveal | Discoveries change decisions, danger, or relationships. | Characters lecture each other or explain facts both already know. |
| Episode architecture | Every proposed unit has required spine beats, a distinct job, entry/exit state, turn, and causal transition. | Plot is mechanically split by quota, length, or date, or omits required coverage. |
| Pacing | Conflict begins early and each unit materially advances the series. | Repeated exposition, static discussion, or redundant beats stall progress. |
| Screenability | Essential meaning is observable or audible through performance and environment. | Interior prose or abstract explanation cannot be played or filmed. |
| Dialogue | Dialogue has intent, interaction, differentiated pressure, and room for subtext. | History-textbook exposition dominates or voices are interchangeable. |
| Historical integrity | New claims respect the Work evidence boundary and preserve uncertainty. | Key facts drift or unverified adaptation claims become fact. |
| Climax and payoff | The climax follows accumulated choices and resolves the central conflict at real cost. | Climax is missing, accidental, or unrelated to prior escalation. |
| Ending fidelity | The ending fulfills or deliberately complicates the Work premise, theme, and arcs. | The ending changes the approved destination or abandons the arc. |
| Downstream readiness | Episode development can elaborate units without inventing the whole-series logic. | Episode purpose, main line, arc direction, or ending remains undefined. |

## Reject common anti-patterns

- **Plot Summary:** narrate the story broadly without a screen progression or episode logic.
- **Event List:** use historical chronology as the dramatic line.
- **Mechanical Episode Split:** divide material evenly without distinct dramatic jobs and state changes.
- **Exposition Dialogue:** make characters teach history instead of pursue conflicting objectives.
- **Passive Protagonist:** let history push the lead through units without consequential decisions.
- **Flat Escalation:** repeat conflicts without higher cost or narrower choices.
- **Repeated Beats:** replay the same political discussion, accusation, or argument.
- **Disconnected Subplots:** retain secondary lines that do not affect the main progression.
- **Character Drift:** detach action and motivation from the approved Work.
- **Historical Drift:** alter the approved evidence boundary or key fact for convenience.
- **Unfilmable Interior Prose:** rely on thoughts and abstract statements with no observable form.
- **Premature Scene Detail:** write exhaustive Scene action/dialogue or camera/Shot instructions at Script level.

## Classify the failure before revising

Use local revision for limited dialogue, an isolated beat, small exposition burden, a minor unit ordering issue, or contained continuity defect when the adaptation contract and structure remain sound.

Re-plan the Script when the main line, motivation, arc translation, escalation, episode architecture, climax/payoff, ending fidelity, or screenability strategy fails. Rewrite the full draft when structural failures affect several connected areas.

When a failure originates in premise, protagonist, historical boundary, or another Work foundation, label it as an upstream Work issue. Do not silently “fix” it by creating a different Script, and do not automatically invoke another Skill.

After every revision or re-plan, apply the entire rubric again. Never move directly from a fix to persistence.

## Persist gate

Allow `script.create_script` or `script.save_script` only after all rubric rows pass and the candidate is a screen-adaptable formal Script state. Reject persistence for a plot summary, event list, few-line episode outline, passive main line, absent visible arcs, flat escalation, missing climax, mechanical allocation, unfilmable prose, or material Work drift.

Keep adaptation alternatives, draft reasoning, review findings, and revision notes in temporary working state. Persist only the approved adaptation result in the existing Stable Envelope and open Domain Content.
