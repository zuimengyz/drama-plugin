# Work Review and Revision

Apply this rubric to the complete Work draft before persistence. Treat every row as critical: one unresolved failure means Review FAIL.

## Domain rubric

| Check | PASS evidence | FAIL signal |
|---|---|---|
| Story identity | A distinct dramatic proposition organizes the history. | An event summary, encyclopedia entry, or chronology stands in for a story. |
| Protagonist | A clear dramatic center makes consequential choices. | The protagonist is absent, passive, or merely witnesses history. |
| Motivation | External goal and internal need/blind spot generate action. | Motivation is generic, retrospective, or detached from decisions. |
| Opposition | An intelligible force has its own objective, logic, and capacity to act. | “Power struggle” is only a label or the opponent is a flat villain. |
| Stakes | Failure has concrete, escalating personal and wider consequences. | Nothing material changes if the protagonist fails. |
| Dramatic causality | Choice causes consequence, escalation, and narrower options. | Events are joined mainly by date or historical sequence. |
| Character arc | Pressure and irreversible choices produce a legible final change. | The draft only asserts that someone “grows” or “becomes resolute.” |
| Relationship arc | A core relationship changes through conflict and choice. | Relationships are static biographies or a detached relationship chart. |
| Theme | A dramatic question is tested by action and consequence. | Theme is a label or moral pasted onto the plot. |
| Structure | Start, disruption, escalation, reversal, crisis, climax, and ending form a causal whole. | The story lacks a turn, climax, payoff, or final state. |
| Historical integrity | Supported, disputed, and invented material remain distinguishable. | Key outcome/causality drifts, uncertainty becomes fact, or invention contradicts evidence. |
| Short-form suitability | Scope, density, and turns fit the intended audience and format. | The design is diffuse, repetitive, or impossible within the chosen scale. |
| Downstream readiness | Script can inherit the foundation without redesigning it. | Premise, center, arcs, boundary, climax, or ending must still be invented downstream. |

## Reject common anti-patterns

- **Historical Summary:** recount the event but establish no protagonist-driven dramatic proposition.
- **Chronology Dump:** list dates or episodes without choice-and-consequence causality.
- **Character List:** describe people without goals, opposition, or relationship change.
- **Theme Label:** name “power,” “loyalty,” or another topic without a tested question.
- **Conflict Label:** mention court struggle or war without opposing objectives and action.
- **No Stakes:** leave failure without a concrete cost.
- **Passive Protagonist:** let history move the central character without consequential choice.
- **Villain Flattening:** remove the opposition's rational goal and historical complexity.
- **Historical Drift:** alter a key fact, outcome, presence, or causality for convenience.
- **Over-Scripting:** replace Work architecture with Scene dialogue, beat-by-beat action, or Shot detail.

## Classify the failure before revising

Use local revision only for isolated wording, clarity, labeling, or minor consistency problems that do not change the selected story engine.

Re-plan the Work when protagonist agency, premise, central conflict, opposition, stakes, thematic question, causal architecture, climax, arc, short-form shape, or historical boundary fails. Rewrite the full draft when structural failures affect multiple connected areas; do not patch each symptom independently.

After every revision or re-plan, apply the entire rubric again. Never move directly from a fix to persistence.

## Persist gate

Allow `work.create_work` or `work.save_work` only after all rubric rows pass and the draft is a formal story foundation. Reject persistence when the candidate contains only an event summary, theme, character list, timeline, one-line premise, passive protagonist, unopposed goal, missing stakes, absent climax/ending, or unresolved historical boundary.

Keep plan alternatives, draft reasoning, review findings, and revision notes in temporary working state. Persist only the approved creative result in the existing Stable Envelope and open Domain Content.
